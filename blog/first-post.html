The Decision to Buy vs. Build

The decision to buy vs. build has been a challenge for technology and finance teams, even before the era of artificial intelligence. Ultimately, do we buy this off the shelf and have it up and running in production in a few weeks, or do we build this ourselves with our internal tools and team? That question has become easier in recent years with the AI coding tools available today. In my professional career, especially at a mid-size brokerage where it's important to be cost conscious, we were able to manage early growth with buying off-the-shelf products and tools available in the marketplace. Our technology stack had a fair number of partners, and we were able to rely on those partners to help us stand up the products quickly. As we matured, we started down this path of, can we start building some of these tools ourselves? That is where we saw the fork in the road, where AI began to give my team the capability to deploy production-ready tools at our company between operations, accounting, and sales.

It all started with the possibilities my team was able to deliver using AI as a tool. Many of the projects in the beginning were scrappy, low-code solutions, but they were tools that worked. These tools helped improve our operational workflow. Projects in the past that would be nice to have and not worth exploring five years ago were now being built in-house with the agility of our technology team sitting directly with operations to understand what they needed. In the past projects would have cost thousands of dollars to stand up using a third-party vendor, we were now delivering production solutions ourselves. Many off-the-shelf tools are not specific to a company's workflow, and those workflows are where you tend to create your competitive moat. When you empower a team to start building on their own, you can start to create tools specific to your team's domain.

An impactful project was a portal for our team to vet and verify trucking providers coming into our network. Our team wanted to create a portal that allowed for higher visibility into the carriers and which started to allow our team to capture data on what attributes would flag to make a "qualify" or "disqualify" decision. It was a quick win and thoughtful build, with a few weeks of iteration, the portal was up and running for our team.

Yes, there were tools in the marketplace we could have chosen, but I found many of the solutions offered were too robust or complicated for what we were looking for. The question needs to be asked, "What is the upside?" There are projects we're working on that vendors can build for us or buy off-the-shelf, but if we take those projects in-house, we can stand them up quicker and iterate faster. That has a lot of value. At the beginning of these conversations, I was looking at it through a lens of how can we save money by doing this ourselves? That mindset shifted to asking, what can build internally to improve our own workflows we become accustomed to over the years? There is more value in that thought process versus saving money and reducing headcount. The goal then became empowering employees with in-house tools and solutions.

My goal started to become focused on deleting processes. Over time, companies get weighed down with operational inefficiencies. Activities creep into the workflow scope just to get things done and complete the task at hand. Over time those compound, and you start looking at workflows under a magnifying glass and asking, "why are we doing this this way?" Asking why enough times gets you deep enough to understand what processes are necessary and what can be deleted.

If you are in a role where you have the domain knowledge and ability to spec and scope out a workflow, my recommendation is to try to build it. It does take time to get things right, but you can fail quickly, iterate, learn and understand quickly. The opportunity to fail fast and learn is moving at such a rate with the AI tools available, I suggest you experiment. There's a lot you can do once you start engaging with these tools. It does not have to be large, architecture heavy projects, think more about delivering quick wins and be able to measure results. Watching the success of a tool you've built internally is extremely rewarding and that is what has kept me at the forefront of continuing to improve and enhance our user's workflow, delete processes, and further widen our moat.
